Changsha Without the Chili: What to Order If You Can't Handle Spice

By Shao Diefeichangsha non spicy food

You can eat well in Changsha without eating spicy food. It takes slightly more care, but the city has plenty of mild options — and locals eat them too.

What to Say

  • 不要辣 (bú yào là) — no chili, please. The most useful phrase you will learn.
  • 微辣 (wēi là) — a little spicy. Still noticeable.
  • 一点辣 (yī diǎn là) — just a tiny bit.

Say it when you order, not after the food arrives. Most stalls cook to order and will happily leave the chili out.

Reliably Mild Dishes

  • Sugar Oil Baba (糖油粑粑) — sweet, fried, no chili at all.
  • Rice noodles in clear broth (清汤米粉) — ask for the clear soup, not the red one.
  • Steamed buns and dumplings — widely available, mild, cheap.
  • Milk tea — Changsha's local chains are excellent and completely safe.
  • Grilled skewers without chili powder — ask for 不辣; cumin-only is common and delicious.
  • Fried rice cake without chili — chewy, savory, mild.

What to Watch Out For

Some dishes are spicy by construction — the sauce is made in advance and cannot be adjusted. If you point at something in a big pot of red chili oil, it will be spicy no matter what you say. When unsure, point at ingredients rather than finished dishes.

The Honest Bottom Line

You will eat slightly less variety than a chili-tolerant traveler. You will not go hungry. See also our guide to how spicy Hunan food actually is.
